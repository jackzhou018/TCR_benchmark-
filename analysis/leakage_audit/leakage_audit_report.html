TCR–pMHC Leakage Audit

Every target in the benchmark was checked against 259 experimentally determined TCR–pMHC complexes released on or before 30 September 2021. No target breaches the temporal cutoff. Sequence redundancy is another matter: 10 complexes are near-duplicates of a pre-cutoff entry on every chain, 42 carry a redundant TCR, and the 126 targets collapse to 66 independent cases.

All-chain similarity

Redundancy within the benchmark

Targets clustered by concatenated Vα+Vβ ≥90%, or either chain ≥95% on the same pMHC. 135 redundant pairs, 18 of them single-residue peptide variants of each other. 87 targets sit in a multi-member cluster; the largest holds 11.
66independent clusters
Test 5 does not discriminate here. TCR–pMHC complexes are architecturally stereotyped, so whole-complex TM-score sits at a median of 0.895 across the entire benchmark regardless of sequence. Of the 54 targets over 0.90, 44 match a reference whose peptide is under 50% identical and 48 match one whose TCR is under 90% identical; TM-score correlates with peptide identity at Spearman ρ = 0.14. Treat it as corroboration for a sequence-flagged target, never as a redundancy criterion on its own.

Does the leakage show up in the predictions?

identity vs Global DockQ

Yes, but weakly, and not through the channel you would expect. Peptide and MHC identity to the closest pre-cutoff complex predict nothing (ρ ≈ 0.04–0.20). TCR Vβ identity predicts a little (ρ ≈ 0.29–0.37 for all four methods). What predicts accuracy best is the structural resemblance of the target itself to the pre-cutoff record — the same multimer TM-score that was useless as a redundancy criterion is the strongest correlate of accuracy in the audit (ρ = 0.38 to 0.55).

The gap is real

Median Global DockQ on the 10 near-duplicates versus the 42 clean targets: AF3 0.765 vs 0.604 (Δ +0.16, CI +0.00 to +0.32), Protenix 0.788 vs 0.530 (+0.26, +0.05 to +0.41), ESMFold2 0.883 vs 0.655 (+0.23), TCRmodel2 0.790 vs 0.626 (+0.16). Reported accuracy on this benchmark is inflated by the redundant tail.

But it is not template retrieval

ESMFold2 shows the effect as strongly as anyone (ρ = 0.45 on TM-score, the largest near-duplicate lift of the four) and it receives no MSA and no structural template — only the query sequence. Whatever drives the gradient is not a template lookup.

A confounder, not a verdict

TM-score to the closest pre-cutoff complex measures how canonical a target's structure is. Canonical complexes are both well represented in the old record and intrinsically easier to predict, so this correlation cannot separate memorisation from typicality. The sequence-identity correlations, which can, are the weak ones.

What would separate them

Accuracy on targets that are structurally canonical but sequence-novel, against targets that are sequence-close but structurally unusual. The benchmark has too few of the second group (10 near-duplicates, 4 of them sharing one reference) to run that contrast at useful power.

What the priors look like

test 4 · all 126 targets

Same MHC, another peptide — 115

Near-universal. The pre-cutoff record covers the common alleles densely, so almost every target's MHC has been seen in the groove-bearing conformation with some other peptide.

Binary pMHC, no ternary complex — 50

The exact peptide (≥90% identity) already sits in a pre-cutoff structure with the same MHC and no TCR: the composite is new, the epitope is not. Found by searching RCSB for pre-cutoff entries with ≤3 polymer entities whose MHC matches at ≥95%.

Similar TCR, another pMHC — 32

Concatenated Vα+Vβ ≥80% to a pre-cutoff complex whose peptide is under 50% identical — the receptor is familiar, its target is not.

Same or highly similar pMHC — 25

MHC ≥90% and peptide ≥90% to a pre-cutoff ternary complex. The docking problem is what remains genuinely unseen for these.

Method and limits

what to trust
Reference set
259 TCR–pMHC complexes released on or before 2021-09-30, assembled from the TCR3d class I and class II tables and the STCRDab all-structures summary (filtered to TCRtype = abTCR, mhc_type ∈ {MH1, MH2}), cropped to the benchmark's own domain boundaries — 1,112 chains, 258 complete complexes. This is a proxy: no structure predictor publishes its actual training list.
Identity
Global Needleman–Wunsch, BLOSUM62, gap −11/−1 (Biopython PairwiseAligner), identity over the shorter sequence, ≥80% coverage of both required for protein chains. Peptides are aligned directly with no coverage gate. 21,549 target × reference chain-set comparisons.
Dates
RCSB Data API entry records, rcsb_accession_info.deposit_date and initial_release_date. All 126 release dates agree with the repository's manifest.
Structure
Foldseek-Multimer 10.941cd33, easy-multimersearch --exact-tmscore 1, native PDBs versus references cropped to the same domains and relabelled to benchmark chain letters.
Coverage gap
213 of 259 references could be cropped cleanly; 46 were skipped where the recorded author chain or residue span did not resolve in the deposited coordinates. Tests 1–4 and 6 use all 259 — only test 5 is affected.
Not tested
Whether any predictor actually trained on these entries; MSA-level leakage (a target's own sequences appearing in the MSAs supplied at inference); and template leakage beyond the explicit template-date cutoffs the runs already applied.
Audit run read-only against benchmark_fresh · reference set from analysis/training_sequence_similarity/ · no repository file was modified. Databases: RCSB PDB (Data API, Search API, GraphQL), TCR3d, STCRDab. Tools: Foldseek-Multimer (Kim et al., 2024), Biopython PairwiseAligner, BLOSUM62.
