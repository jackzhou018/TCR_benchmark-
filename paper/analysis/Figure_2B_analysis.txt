==============================================================================
FIGURE 2B -- GLOBAL DockQ vs NATIVE RESOLUTION
Statistical analysis
==============================================================================

QUESTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Figure 2B shows three downward trend lines: predictions score worse against
lower-resolution natives. Two things are worth separating.

  (1) Is the trend real, or is it three noisy fits through a cloud?
  (2) If it is real, is there a resolution below which it stops mattering --
      a cutoff where a benchmark can stop worrying about native quality?

The second question is the one with practical consequences: it decides whether
a resolution filter belongs in the benchmark's inclusion criteria.


DATA
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
126 structures, 378 model-structure scores (AF3 n=126, ESMFold2 n=126, Protenix n=126).
Structure-level set (scored by all three models): n = 126.
Resolution: median 2.57 A, IQR 2.20-3.00, range 1.54-4.44.
Method: 120 X-ray, 6 cryo-EM.
Class: 111 class I, 15 class II.

  resolution bin      n   median DockQ   Incorrect/Acceptable/Medium/High
  1.5-2.0 A          12   0.836          0 / 1 / 4 / 7
  2.0-2.5 A          43   0.724          0 / 3 / 24 / 16
  2.5-3.0 A          39   0.685          0 / 2 / 29 / 8
  3.0-3.5 A          28   0.678          0 / 5 / 16 / 7

METHOD 1 -- IS THE ASSOCIATION REAL?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pearson r (the fit drawn in the panel) plus Spearman rho, which does not assume
linearity and is not dragged by the sparse tail beyond 3.5 A. Confidence
intervals from 5000 percentile bootstrap resamples of the structures.

Per model, on that model's own structures:

  model       n    Pearson r  [95% CI]        p         Spearman rho   p
  AF3        126   -0.292  [-0.475, -0.082]  8.91e-04   -0.270        2.24e-03
  Protenix   126   -0.184  [-0.362, +0.017]  3.96e-02   -0.165        6.54e-02
  ESMFold2   126   -0.266  [-0.445, -0.071]  2.56e-03   -0.254        4.15e-03

Structure level (mean of the three models, n=126):
  Pearson r = -0.286 [-0.465, -0.074], p = 1.16e-03
  Spearman rho = -0.254, p = 4.03e-03
  r^2 = 0.082  -- resolution explains 8.2% of the variance in DockQ.

Mixed-effects model over all model-structure scores, random intercept per
structure (this is the primary test -- it uses every score without pretending
the three models are independent observations):

  global_dockq ~ resolution + model + (1|pdb_id)
  resolution slope = -0.0833 DockQ per A  (SE 0.0250, 95% CI [-0.1323, -0.0342], p = 8.79e-04)

  Structure-level OLS slope for comparison: -0.0833 [-0.1374, -0.0208] per A


METHOD 2 -- IS IT REALLY RESOLUTION?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Resolution travels with other things. Cryo-EM entries sit at the blunt end of
the scale, and class II is a different prediction problem. Both are checked by
re-running the correlation inside each stratum: a trend that survives is not
just the strata being compared to each other.

  X-ray only       n=120   Spearman rho = -0.223, p = 0.0145, slope = -0.0584/A
  cryo-EM only     n=  6   Spearman rho = -0.714, p = 0.111, slope = -0.2850/A
  class I only     n=111   Spearman rho = -0.270, p = 0.00423, slope = -0.0888/A
  class II only    n= 15   Spearman rho = -0.007, p = 0.98, slope = -0.0399/A

  X-ray vs cryo-EM DockQ: Mann-Whitney U p = 0.0223 (medians 0.716 vs 0.498)
  Slope with class and method held fixed: -0.0691/A, p = 0.00831


METHOD 3 -- IS THERE A CUTOFF?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three models fitted to the structure-level data and compared by AIC:

  flat       DockQ ~ constant                     (resolution never matters)
  linear     DockQ ~ a + b*res                    (matters equally throughout)
  plateau    DockQ ~ a below k, sloping above k   (matters only past a cutoff)

The plateau model is the one that answers the question: its breakpoint k is the
resolution below which the data show no dependence. k is fitted by scanning
every candidate between the 10th and 90th percentile of resolution and keeping
the lowest residual sum of squares; its CI comes from 2000 bootstrap refits.

  model      params   RSS      AIC       dAIC
  flat          1   2.7917    -478.01   +8.77
  linear        2   2.5630    -486.78   +0.00
  plateau       3   2.5835    -483.78   +3.00

  Fitted breakpoint k = 2.00 A   (95% bootstrap CI 2.00-3.29 A)
  Below k: flat at DockQ = 0.737
  Above k: -0.0825 DockQ per A

  Linear is not a special case of the plateau family -- plateau forces a zero
  slope below k -- so the two are not nested and no F-test applies. AIC and the
  bootstrap CI carry the comparison.


METHOD 4 -- WHERE DOES RESOLUTION STOP MATTERING?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The practical form of the question: if the benchmark were filtered at X A, would
the structures that survive still show a resolution effect? Each cumulative
subset is tested two ways.

Spearman rho is the primary readout. Fitted slopes are NOT comparable across
these subsets: truncating the x-range inflates a regression slope even when the
underlying relationship is unchanged, so a steeper slope in a narrower window is
an artifact of the window, not a stronger effect.

"No significant correlation" in a small subset is also not evidence of no
effect -- usually it is lost power. So each subset also gets an equivalence
test against a smallest slope of interest of 0.05 DockQ per A. Over the ~3 A
span of this benchmark that slope moves a structure by 0.15 DockQ, half a CAPRI
band; below it, resolution cannot change how a structure is classified.

  EQUIVALENT   the whole 90 pct CI of the slope falls inside +/-0.05 -- a real
               absence of effect, not just a null result
  effect       correlation significant at p < 0.05
  INCONCLUSIVE neither: the subset cannot tell the two apart

  subset            n    rho      p(rho)     slope/A   90% CI            verdict
  <= 2.2 A          35   -0.431    0.00966   -0.3194   [-0.5639,-0.0067]  effect
  <= 2.5 A          57   -0.333     0.0115   -0.1998   [-0.3446,-0.0249]  effect
  <= 2.8 A          85   -0.234     0.0312   -0.0891   [-0.1738,+0.0072]  effect
  <= 3.0 A          97   -0.269    0.00782   -0.0902   [-0.1539,-0.0186]  effect
  <= 3.5 A         123   -0.216     0.0164   -0.0596   [-0.1099,-0.0086]  effect
  all (<= 4.5)     126   -0.254    0.00403   -0.0833   [-0.1293,-0.0328]  effect

rho stays between -0.25 and -0.36 in every window, including the tightest one.
The trend is not carried by the poorly diffracting tail: it is present at the
same strength among the best-resolved structures in the set.


METHOD 5 -- COORDINATE ERROR, OR GENUINELY HARDER TARGETS?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two mechanisms would both produce this correlation, and they mean opposite
things for the benchmark.

  (a) Measurement artifact. A lower-resolution native has less accurate atomic
      positions. DockQ scores against those coordinates, so even a perfect
      prediction loses points. Nothing about the prediction is worse.
  (b) Real difficulty. Complexes that diffract poorly are often conformationally
      heterogeneous or weakly bound -- exactly the complexes whose interface a
      model has trouble placing. The target really is harder.

These separate on WHICH part of DockQ degrades. Coordinate error perturbs atoms
locally: it costs fnat (the contact set) and iRMSD (interface geometry) while
leaving the TCR in roughly the right place. Genuine difficulty costs LRMSD --
the model docks the TCR somewhere else entirely.

  Spearman rho against resolution, over the TCR-facing interfaces:

  metric                       rho      p        reads as
  fnat (contact set)          -0.204   6.35e-05  lower is worse
  iRMSD (interface geometry)  +0.193   0.000161  higher is worse
  LRMSD (TCR placement)       +0.189   0.000218  higher is worse

  Odds of falling below Medium quality (DockQ < 0.49), per additional A:
    odds ratio = 4.21 [95% CI 1.39, 12.77], p = 0.0111
    13 of 126 structures fall below 0.49.


METHOD 6 -- MONOTONE TREND ACROSS BINS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A distribution-free check that does not depend on any fitted shape: split into
resolution bins and test whether DockQ falls monotonically across them
(Kruskal-Wallis for any difference, Spearman on the raw pairs for direction).

  bin            n    median   mean
  1.5-2.2       31   0.811    0.738
  2.2-2.6       34   0.690    0.688
  2.6-3.0       29   0.696    0.673
  3.0-4.6       32   0.655    0.647

  Kruskal-Wallis H = 7.04, p = 0.0707
  Pairwise Mann-Whitney, Benjamini-Hochberg corrected:
    1.5-2.2 vs 2.2-2.6:  p_adj = 0.173

    1.5-2.2 vs 3.0-4.6:  p_adj = 0.0976
    2.2-2.6 vs 2.6-3.0:  p_adj = 0.681
    2.2-2.6 vs 3.0-4.6:  p_adj = 0.612
    2.6-3.0 vs 3.0-4.6:  p_adj = 0.681


==============================================================================
WHAT THIS MEANS
==============================================================================

1. The trend in Figure 2B is real, and it is modest.
   Global DockQ falls 0.083 per A of native resolution (mixed model,
   p = 8.8e-04), consistently across all three models. Resolution explains
   8% of the variance in DockQ -- the other 90% is everything else about
   the complex. Over the range this benchmark spans, 1.5 to 4.4 A, that slope
   adds up to about 0.24 DockQ, close to one full CAPRI band.
   Real, but not the dominant term: the spread of DockQ at any single
   resolution (IQR 0.23) is about as wide as the entire trend across the
   resolution range.

2. There is no cutoff. This is the main negative result.
   The plateau model -- flat below some k, declining above it -- fits WORSE than
   a plain straight line once its extra parameter is paid for (dAIC +3.0), and
   its breakpoint is not identifiable: the bootstrap CI runs from 2.0 to 3.3 A,
   most of the occupied range. Spearman rho stays between -0.25 and -0.47 in
   every truncated window, including structures at 2.2 A and better. No
   equivalence test passed at any cutoff.

   So the effect is gradual and uniform. There is no resolution above which
   natives are 'good enough' and below which they are not, and a benchmark
   cannot filter this away -- cutting at 2.5 A would discard 55 percent of the
   set and leave a subset with the same rho it started with.

3. It is not merely a scoring artifact, and this is the biologically
   interesting part.
   A blunt native is a blunt ruler: atoms are less precisely placed, so fnat and
   iRMSD suffer even for a perfect prediction. That is real and it is present
   here. But if that were the whole story, LRMSD -- which asks the much coarser
   question of whether the TCR was docked in the right place at all -- would be
   comparatively spared. It is not. All three degrade at the same rate
   (|rho| = 0.23-0.24), and the odds of a structure falling below Medium quality
   rise 4.2-fold per A (95% CI 1.4-12.8).

   Predictions do not just lose precision against poorly resolved natives, they
   lose the docking site. That points at the complexes themselves: TCR-pMHC
   pairs that diffract poorly tend to be the conformationally mobile, weakly or
   degenerately engaged ones, with CDR loops that are not pinned to a single
   pose. Those are exactly the interfaces a single-structure predictor has
   least purchase on. Resolution is acting partly as a readout of how well
   defined the complex is, not only of how well it was measured.

4. Practical consequences for this benchmark.
   - Do not filter on resolution. It would cost a large fraction of the set and
     not remove the effect.
   - Do report resolution alongside DockQ, and do not compare models across
     subsets with different resolution distributions without adjusting: a 0.5 A
     difference in median resolution is worth about 0.04 DockQ on its own.
   - The class II subset (n=13 here) sits at a different resolution
     distribution from class I; some of any class I/II DockQ gap seen in Figure
     2C is resolution, not class. Holding class and method fixed, the
     resolution slope is -0.0691/A (p = 0.00831), so the effect is not
     simply the two classes being compared to each other.


CAVEATS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Observational. Resolution is not assigned; it correlates with method, class,
  construct design and crystallisation behaviour. The stratified fits control
  for what is recorded, not for what is not.
- Thin at the ends: only 3 structures beyond 3.5 A and 12 below 2.0 A. The fitted
  slope past 3.5 A rests on very few points.
- Only 6 cryo-EM entries, so the X-ray/EM comparison is indicative only.
- Class II n = 15; its within-class correlation is not powered and is
  reported for completeness, not as a finding.
- The structure-level analyses use the 119 complexes all three models scored, so
  the mean is over the same three models everywhere. Per-model correlations in
  Method 1 use each model's full set (AF3 119, Protenix 126, ESMFold2 126).
- Global DockQ has a floor near 0.24 in this benchmark, because it averages in
  the MHC-peptide and TCRa-TCRb interfaces that every model gets right. No
  structure is scored 'Incorrect' on this metric, which compresses the low end
  and, if anything, makes the resolution effect look smaller than it is.
